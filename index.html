The fourth pair is where the cheap work pants lose

Work pants die on a schedule, at a known address: the knee goes first, and the thigh seam and cuffs queue behind it. That is why the durable review ranks them by how they fail, and why sticker price is the wrong unit. Cotton canvas is sold by the ounce for a reason: a basic work pant runs about nine-ounce canvas, while the double-knee tier wears twelve-ounce duck with a second layer sewn exactly where the first layer dies.

Set the sticker aside and price the drawer instead. A $25 basic pair on an eight-month failure cadence costs $37.50 a year. The crossover against a $90 double-knee is the fourth pair: one hundred dollars spent at month twenty-four, and every year after that the cheap drawer pays another $37.50 while the $90 pair is still holding. Hold the heavy pair three years and the ledger reads $90 against $112.50, a $22.50 margin that widens with every faster cadence.

The cadence is the dial. A six-month wear-out moves the crossover to month eighteen; three months pulls it to month nine. Price per ounce reads backward on purpose: $2.78 an ounce for the basic canvas against $7.50 for the duck, because the extra $65 is the doubled panel at the failure address, which is repairability you never have to schedule.

Light duty never reaches the crossover inside one garment's life, so the cheap pant stays honest there. The failure cadence is the unit that decides, and the ranking that prices it is the work pants ranked by how they fail at DurablePicks.
